# Ternhaven Housing, Workshop & Social Life v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Status:** Working proposal bounded by World & System Integration Guardrails  
**Initial scope:** Ternhaven only

## Purpose

Give the player a growing sense that Ternhaven becomes **home** without turning Adventurer Life RPG into a property simulator.

Housing extends systems already present:
- inn/rest;
- collection;
- crafting;
- Signature Gear;
- local standing;
- player social spaces;
- Ternhaven market/Guild.

---

# 1. Housing progression

Housing progression represents stability and belonging, not combat level.

## Stage 0 — Guild/Inn Bed
Available from the beginning.

Functions:
- rest;
- save/return fiction;
- basic storage through Guild/inn services.

## Stage 1 — Rented Room
Plausible after the player has steady work and can afford rent/deposit.

Functions:
- personal storage;
- equipment display;
- small decoration;
- bookshelf/journal space;
- invite party/friends.

## Stage 2 — Long-term Lease / Small Home
Later local commitment.

Functions:
- more display;
- small cooking/garden space;
- one bounded craft workspace where appropriate;
- social hosting.

## Stage 3 — Specialist Home/Workshop
For players who choose artisan/life progression.

Examples:
- smith's leased workspace near Bellcross-style trade services;
- apothecary room with safe preparation bench;
- cartographer study;
- cook's kitchen.

This is specialization, not a mandatory upgrade.

---

# 2. Why Ternhaven housing exists

Ternhaven is a trade/Guild/dungeon city.

Plausible housing providers:
- innkeeper rooms;
- ordinary landlords;
- craft-yard leases;
- civic/Guild-referred lodging for licensed adventurers.

The Adventurer Guild may verify identity or mediate deposits.

The Guild does **not** own every house.

---

# 3. No land scarcity gameplay

Shared-world direction requires avoiding permanent land monopoly.

Initial technical direction:

- housing interior can be instanced/personal;
- neighborhood/exterior can remain shared;
- multiple players can lease equivalent units;
- no first-come-only plots required for core housing.

Prestige comes from what the player displays and has done, not owning the only useful plot.

---

# 4. Housing functions

Approved working functions:

## Rest / return
A stable personal return point within normal travel rules.

Not teleportation across the world.

## Storage
Personal organization.

Storage expansion is a money sink but must remain reasonable.

## Display
Show:
- Signature Gear;
- relic replicas/originals where safe;
- maps;
- trophies;
- books;
- fish/collection records;
- expedition memories.

## Social
Invite:
- party;
- friends;
- customers for a commission discussion.

## Craft support
One small appropriate workspace.

Heavy industrial operations remain at real workshops.

## Gardening
Small planters/garden for:
- herbs;
- cooking ingredients;
- decorative plants;
- Grey Orchard research where story permits.

This is not mass agriculture.

---

# 5. Gardening boundary

Aldermead remains a real agricultural community.

Player gardening must not make farmers economically pointless.

Home garden output is:
- low volume;
- personal;
- specialist;
- decorative;
- experimental.

Bulk grain, wool, livestock and regional food still come from the world economy.

---

# 6. Workshop boundary

Home/workshop does not passively craft while the player is offline.

Allowed:
- store tools/materials;
- perform small recipes;
- take commissions;
- display work;
- repair appropriate gear.

Large forge, heavy smelting and specialist ancient restoration may require:
- Bellcross smithy;
- licensed workshop;
- specialist facility;
- hidden-realm station.

This keeps settlements relevant.

---

# 7. Player shop model

Do not create an AFK NPC shop empire.

Three existing channels cover most needs:

1. **Common Exchange** — ordinary asynchronous market.
2. **Commission system** — custom work.
3. **Optional personal display/order board** — advertises what the player makes.

A home workshop can show sample work and accepted commission types.

Sales settle through existing market/escrow rules.

No passive NPC worker producing wealth.

---

# 8. Social reputation — reuse existing concepts

Do NOT add one giant "Social Level."

Use context:

## Guild standing / professional evidence
For adventurer conduct.

## Local standing
For settlement relationships.

## Artisan reputation
For craft/service work.

## Personal relationships
For named NPC/companion stories.

A helpful blacksmith can be locally respected without becoming a higher Guild Rank.

---

# 9. Neighbor/community play

Shared-world social goals:

- visit a friend's room/home;
- inspect displayed collections;
- commission equipment;
- share a meal;
- plan an expedition;
- exchange maps/knowledge where rules allow;
- small gatherings.

Avoid:
- required neighborhood politics;
- homeowners' guild bureaucracy;
- mandatory voting;
- land warfare.

---

# 10. Home progression rewards

Prefer:
- new display capacity;
- decoration;
- furniture;
- workspace options;
- garden options;
- storage;
- social hosting;
- cosmetic identity.

Avoid:
- permanent attack bonus because the player owns a bed;
- exclusive combat skill locked behind expensive housing;
- daily login housing rewards.

---

# 11. Relationship to collections

Housing gives collections a physical meaning.

Examples:
- wall map gradually updated by The Unfinished Atlas;
- Signature Gear rack;
- relic cabinet;
- recipe bookshelf;
- fishing display;
- expedition table with route tokens.

Collection remains biography, not checklist power.

---

# 12. Relationship to the Guild

The Guild can:
- refer new adventurers to lodging;
- authenticate commission identity;
- provide expedition notices;
- record a recognized craft/restoration achievement.

The Guild does not:
- determine interior decoration;
- confiscate homes for low merit;
- convert housing into rank requirement.

---

# 13. Failure/absence policy

Respect players who stop playing.

No:
- house deletion because absent;
- essential items lost to rent arrears;
- crops permanently dying from real-time absence;
- social status decay for not logging in.

If upkeep exists later, it must be forgiving and not threaten owned meaningful items.

---

# 14. Prototype implementation

First housing prototype should contain only:

- one rented Ternhaven room;
- bed/rest interaction;
- storage chest;
- 3–5 display points;
- one desk/work surface;
- small decoration placement;
- invite/visit concept only if shared-world prototype is ready.

Do NOT implement yet:
- land plots;
- multiple cities;
- building construction;
- livestock;
- NPC workers;
- shop automation;
- full farming;
- neighborhood governance.

---

# 15. Example player stories

## Adventurer
Returns from Siltwell, puts the recovered survey token on a shelf, repairs gear and studies tomorrow's route.

## Blacksmith-adventurer
Keeps commission samples on a wall, receives a custom order through escrow, then travels to Bellcross for heavy forge work.

## Cartographer
Uses the room as a study with maps and expedition journals.

## Cook
Collects regional recipes and hosts party members before a journey.

The house supports the same Adventurer Life rather than becoming a separate career-management game.

---

# 16. Acceptance tests

Housing succeeds if:

1. Ternhaven feels more like home;
2. old adventures become visible through objects;
3. crafters/collectors gain meaningful space;
4. non-homeowners are not combat-disadvantaged;
5. no land monopoly exists;
6. settlements/workshops remain relevant;
7. offline absence is not punished;
8. the system can launch with one room and still feel complete enough.
