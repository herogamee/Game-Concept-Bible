# Crafting, Gathering & Artisan Life Bible v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-03
**Status:** Working life-skill/economy proposal
**Depends on:** Economy, Trade/Market, Weapons/Equipment, Signature Gear, Rare Materials

## Core loop

> Discover/Gather -> Process -> Craft -> Use/Trade -> Maintain/Restore -> Recycle/Requisition

Crafting is a life path, not a mandatory side chore.

## 1. Design principles

- no mass-crafting junk as the primary progression method;
- no mandatory daily energy/focus currency;
- no random destruction of valuable equipment;
- material origin matters;
- quality comes from decisions and skill, not only RNG;
- commissions and repair/restoration remain valuable;
- casual players can participate;
- specialists can become socially known;
- gathering respects ecology and world logic.

## 2. Artisan disciplines

Initial families:

### Blacksmithing / Metalwork
Weapons, armor components, tools, repair, reforging.

### Woodcraft / Bowyer
Shafts, bows, handles, furniture, expedition structures.

### Leatherwork / Tailoring
Field wear, armor layers, packs, straps, clothing.

### Alchemy / Apothecary
Medicines, antidotes, preservation, specialist preparations.

### Cooking
Meals, preserved food, expedition rations, cultural dishes.

### Relic Restoration
Historical objects, Pattern interfaces, conservation.

### Cartography / Scribing
Maps, manuals, archive copies, survey products.

Later professions should be added only when they create distinct play.

## 3. Gathering disciplines

- Mining
- Herbalism/Botany
- Forestry
- Fishing
- Foraging
- Salvage
- Monster-material fieldwork

Gathering is observation plus extraction.

## 4. Resource knowledge

A gatherer can learn:

- habitat;
- season;
- signs;
- quality indicators;
- safe harvest method;
- regeneration;
- substitutes;
- contamination;
- associated creatures.

Knowledge can improve yield/quality without simply unlocking a higher numbered node.

## 5. Sustainable harvesting

Resources have ecological rules.

Overharvesting can:
- lower local quality temporarily;
- disturb creatures;
- change contract needs.

Do not let one player permanently ruin a region.

Essential resources need multiple sources/substitutes.

## 6. Material quality

Material batches may vary by:

- purity;
- freshness;
- condition;
- processing;
- provenance.

Avoid excessive micro-grades.

Quality should matter mainly for specialist work, collectables, commissions and high-end restoration.

## 7. Processing / refining

Processing is a real economic step:

- ore -> ingot/alloy;
- timber -> seasoned stock;
- hide -> leather;
- herb -> prepared reagent;
- fish/meat -> preserved ingredient;
- salvage -> sorted reusable component.

Processing choices can trade:
- yield;
- purity;
- time;
- durability;
- specialist property.

## 8. Crafting interaction

A craft has four readable concerns:

1. **Plan** — recipe/design and intended use.
2. **Materials** — properties and substitutions.
3. **Process** — key decisions during work.
4. **Finish** — workmanship, modifications and maker provenance.

Routine recipes can be automated/quick-crafted after mastery.

Important commissions, first crafts, Masterwork attempts and relic work use deeper interaction.

## 9. Workmanship

Ordinary gear uses:
- Worn (existing damaged state, normally not a crafted target)
- Serviceable
- Fine
- Masterwork

Masterwork should result from:
- appropriate skill;
- good materials;
- correct process;
- specialist tools/workshop;
- deliberate decisions.

Not a casino roll.

## 10. Craft progression

Progress comes from a mix of:

- first-time recipes;
- varied commissions;
- difficult repairs;
- material study;
- Guild requisitions;
- mentorship;
- regional methods;
- restoration;
- documented Masterwork;
- discoveries.

Repeating 1,000 identical daggers should rapidly stop teaching meaningful mastery.

## 11. Recipe knowledge

Recipes can come from:

- common training;
- mentor;
- regional tradition;
- experiment;
- manual;
- commission;
- ruin/archive;
- reverse engineering/study;
- hidden realm.

Recipes can support substitutions rather than demanding one exact ingredient forever.

## 12. Commission system

Customer specifies:
- object/function;
- budget;
- preferred material;
- workmanship target;
- modification;
- deadline if relevant.

Crafter provides:
- quote;
- required materials;
- expected result;
- alternatives.

Escrow protects both parties.

## 13. Guild Requisition

The Guild publishes rotating practical demand:
- rescue equipment;
- field coats;
- rope;
- rations;
- bandages;
- replacement tools.

Purpose:
- useful training;
- item sink;
- beginner artisan income;
- world believability.

No daily-login streak required.

## 14. Study / disassembly

A craftsperson may consume an appropriate item to learn:
- construction;
- material behavior;
- modification principle.

Study yields knowledge, not magical duplication.

Relics require conservation rules and are not casually destroyed.

## 15. Maker provenance

Crafted important gear records:
- maker;
- workshop;
- commission owner;
- material where notable;
- date/era;
- later restorers/modifiers.

This integrates with Signature Gear.

## 16. Tools and workshops

Better tools/workshops improve:
- precision;
- safety;
- viable processes;
- efficiency.

They should not simply add +50% success.

Portable tools handle field maintenance.
Complex work requires specialist facilities.

## 17. Failure

Routine crafting after competency should not randomly fail.

Failure belongs mainly to:
- experimentation;
- unsafe substitution;
- advanced restoration;
- deliberately difficult work.

Failure can produce:
- lower yield;
- imperfect result;
- recoverable material loss;
- new knowledge.

Do not destroy irreplaceable player gear without explicit informed risk and recovery path.

## 18. Regional craft identity

Examples:
- Bellcross — metalwork/repair;
- Fenwick — river goods/preservation;
- Highmere — wool/herbs;
- Istrane — trade materials, glass, maritime equipment;
- Ulreth — cold-weather craft.

Regional identity creates travel/trade without exclusive monopolies.

## 19. Artisan reputation

A player can become known for:
- reliability;
- specialization;
- restoration;
- distinctive commissions;
- historical work.

Reputation is not bought directly with gold.

## 20. Prototype scope

First playable implementation:
- Mining
- Herbalism
- Fishing
- Blacksmithing
- Cooking
- Alchemy
- 3 raw resources per gathering path
- 3–5 recipes per craft
- Serviceable/Fine result
- repair
- one commission
- one Guild requisition

Do not build the entire profession tree before core loops feel good.
