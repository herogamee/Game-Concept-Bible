# Main Story & World Mystery v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-02  
**Status:** Narrative working proposal; does not replace the canonical Bible v0.2.1  
**Project:** Adventurer Life RPG

## Purpose

Give the existing Adventurer Life systems a long-form narrative spine without turning the player into a chosen hero at character creation.

The intended progression is:

> **Ordinary adventurer -> trusted professional -> witness to anomalies -> participant in regional conflict -> explorer of world-scale mysteries -> living legend with access to the unknown.**

The Main Story must never erase the value of small local stories. Saving one farm, finding a missing person, mapping a dangerous road or honestly reporting a failed expedition remains meaningful even when world-scale mysteries later appear.

---

## 1. Central mystery — the Underfold is not just a dungeon

Common belief:

> The Underfold is an ancient 100-floor dungeon beneath Ternhaven, filled with monsters, salvage and forgotten ruins.

Evidence gradually contradicts that belief.

The buried structures include:

- pressure-control works;
- water-routing systems;
- drift-regulation patterns;
- gates that behave like infrastructure rather than temples;
- chambers with measurements that do not agree across expeditions;
- architecture that repeats in distant regions;
- old maintenance spaces whose purpose was practical, not ceremonial.

The long-term narrative hypothesis is:

> **The Underfold is one node in a much larger buried world network built to regulate unstable magical and environmental forces.**

This is a working narrative direction, not yet a locked cosmological truth.

---

## 2. The old catastrophe

About 430 years before the present campaign, multiple buried systems failed.

Recorded history is incomplete and politically shaped.

Possible causes remain deliberately unresolved in early content:

1. infrastructure failure;
2. civil war or sabotage among the old builders;
3. misuse of drift regulation;
4. a containment system being deliberately opened;
5. a necessary sacrifice misremembered as a disaster.

The final truth should explain existing observations rather than invalidate them.

Do not introduce a secret royal bloodline or chosen-one ancestry that makes the player's success inevitable.

---

## 3. Drift and world instability

Existing Pattern Magic uses ambient **drift**.

Narrative expansion proposes that excessive or badly routed drift can influence:

- animal migration;
- monster behavior;
- plant growth;
- weather patterns;
- magical reliability;
- underground pressure and water;
- spatial measurements in deep structures;
- rare material formation.

This creates one shared explanation that can connect local quests to the world mystery without making every problem part of the Main Story.

A broken irrigation channel can still simply be a broken irrigation channel.

---

# 4. Rank-based narrative arc

Guild rank remains professional trust, not story level. The following arcs describe the scale of information and responsibility that becomes available, not mandatory linear chapters.

## F — Small lives matter

Player concerns:

- registration;
- food, lodging and equipment;
- first paid work;
- learning to judge risk;
- gaining the trust of ordinary people.

Story signals:

- incorrect water flow;
- misplaced monster habitats;
- unusual slime or insect behavior;
- conflicting route records;
- a veteran returning with an unexplained injury.

Target feeling:

> "The world is much bigger than me."

No prophecy. No world-saving duty.

---

## E — Something is connected

The player begins entering Siltwell and supervised early Underfold areas.

Discoveries may include:

- matching construction marks in separate caves;
- airflow behaving contrary to known tunnels;
- animals displaced from deeper habitats;
- old maintenance tools with unknown symbols;
- Guild reports that have been repeatedly revised.

Target feeling:

> "These incidents are not isolated."

---

## D — The expedition that disagreed with itself

A 27-year-old deep expedition becomes important.

Its surviving maps and witness accounts conflict.

Examples:

- two parties claim to have traversed the same floor without meeting;
- measured distances exceed the exterior geography;
- one archived route appears only under specific pressure conditions;
- an expedition member later withdrew testimony.

The player can investigate the people, institutions and evidence around the event.

Target feeling:

> "The Guild's knowledge is incomplete, and some of it may be deliberately incomplete."

---

## C — Ten interests, one mystery

The ten factions become narratively important.

Different interests may include:

- preserving public safety;
- exploiting rare resources;
- controlling access;
- revealing hidden records;
- protecting religious interpretations;
- maintaining political authority;
- studying old technology;
- preventing another catastrophe;
- opening sealed routes;
- suppressing dangerous knowledge.

No faction should be purely correct or purely evil by default.

The player can earn trust from multiple groups without a binary faction-war lock unless a later story demands it.

Target feeling:

> "Knowledge changes who holds power."

---

## B — The network is larger than Veyr

Travel beyond the starter kingdom reveals parallel structures.

Possible discoveries:

- distant buried gates with related markings;
- regional myths describing the same event differently;
- a port whose tides respond to buried mechanisms;
- a mountain settlement built above an inactive node;
- old trade records showing materials moved between nodes.

At this stage, the player learns that the Underfold is not unique.

Target feeling:

> "The dungeon beneath my first city is one part of a world system."

---

## A — Recorded history cannot explain the evidence

World-scale discoveries begin contradicting accepted history.

Possible questions:

- Did the ancient civilization disappear, migrate, transform or divide?
- Are some current species descended from altered survivors?
- Were certain "monsters" once part of the old system?
- Did the catastrophe save the surface from something worse?
- Are today's Guild laws indirectly inherited from ancient disaster protocols?

The player gains access to expeditions where decisions can affect regions, institutions and relationships.

Target feeling:

> "The past is still operating underneath the present."

---

## S — License to enter the unknown

S is the normal rank ceiling.

S rank should not be a larger damage tier. It grants trust to undertake work for which the Guild cannot honestly guarantee:

- reliable maps;
- rescue;
- complete legal precedent;
- known monster taxonomy;
- political neutrality;
- safe return.

Possible S-level responsibilities:

- world anomalies;
- uncharted buried nodes;
- ancient entities;
- international crises;
- major first discoveries;
- expeditions beyond recognized borders;
- decisions involving sealed knowledge.

Target feeling:

> "I am no longer proving that I am an adventurer. I am deciding what kind of legend I will become."

---

# 5. Floor 100 direction

Do **not** default to:

> Floor 100 -> final demon -> kill -> credits.

Proposed reveal:

**Floor 100 is a control/junction layer rather than the final endpoint.**

It may expose:

- a world map of other buried nodes;
- inactive routes;
- oceanic structures;
- regions missing from modern maps;
- a still-functioning signal;
- one or more sealed destinations.

The exact truth remains open until later narrative design.

The important function is:

> Floor 100 resolves one mystery and opens several deeper ones.

Reaching it should permanently change how the player understands the world.

---

# 6. Story structure — four simultaneous layers

## 6.1 World Mystery

Long-horizon questions:

- What is the buried network?
- Who built it?
- What happened 430 years ago?
- What is drift actually doing?
- Why are deep measurements inconsistent?
- What is Floor 100?

## 6.2 Regional Stories

Each region needs problems that matter even if the Main Story is ignored:

- crop failure;
- migration;
- border dispute;
- flood;
- epidemic;
- smuggling;
- succession;
- religious disagreement;
- trade collapse;
- rescue.

## 6.3 Character Stories

Recurring characters should change over time.

Possible states:

- promotion;
- retirement;
- injury;
- marriage/family;
- changed profession;
- faction allegiance;
- relocation;
- rivalry;
- reconciliation;
- mentorship;
- disappearance.

NPC continuity must remain player-first: characters enrich the player's world rather than autonomously clearing the player's core content.

## 6.4 Player Story

The game should remember meaningful player history:

- first completed contract;
- first justified retreat;
- promotion results;
- discoveries;
- saved settlements;
- failed objectives;
- important relationships;
- named monster encounters;
- major expedition roles.

Do not claim every player is the same single canonical hero.

---

# 7. Mystery design rules

A mystery must leave clues before its answer.

Good chain:

> rumor -> environmental evidence -> conflicting testimony -> archived record -> field proof -> interpretation -> consequence

Avoid:

- random 0.01% trigger with no clue;
- hidden requirement that cannot be inferred;
- retroactive chosen-one reveal;
- lore dump that removes the need to explore;
- one NPC who explains the entire world.

Important discoveries should be understandable after the fact.

---

# 8. Inspiration boundary

The project may study successful techniques from fantasy, isekai, adventure manga/anime, novels and games.

Transferable structures include:

- exams as gateways rather than endings;
- long-term mysteries;
- deep exploration with meaningful return logistics;
- hidden encounters with coherent conditions;
- ecological monsters;
- changing relationships;
- culturally distinct travel;
- society and downtime as gameplay.

Do **not** copy:

- named characters;
- locations;
- factions;
- proprietary power systems;
- signature creatures;
- scenes;
- dialogue;
- maps;
- costumes;
- unique plot twists.

All final lore must remain original IP.

---

# 9. Future Chinese fantasy / xianxia expansion hook

The owner intends a later focused research pass on Chinese fantasy and cultivation fiction, especially stories rich in:

- rare treasures;
- hidden realms;
- manuals and secret techniques;
- inheritance trials;
- ancient ruins;
- unusual materials;
- artifact provenance;
- alchemy/crafting;
- long-horizon personal progression.

These motifs must be translated into original systems compatible with the Adventurer Life world rather than copied as cultivation realms or named techniques.

A future research pass should specifically study how to make **treasures, techniques and secret realms feel discoverable and valuable without turning the entire game into vertical power inflation**.

---

# 10. Narrative guardrails

- Start personal before becoming epic.
- Small civilian outcomes continue to matter at high rank.
- S rank opens uncertainty; it does not end the game.
- Main Dungeon progress is primarily player/community driven.
- Major bosses and first discoveries remain player-facing.
- No autonomous NPC party should complete the central mystery off-screen.
- Avoid mandatory daily attendance to keep the world understandable for players with limited time.
- Mystery should reward attention, not wiki dependency.
