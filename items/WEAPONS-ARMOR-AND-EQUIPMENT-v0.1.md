# Weapons, Armor & Signature Equipment Bible v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Status:** Working equipment-system proposal for v0.3 validation  
**Depends on:** Skills & Mastery, Treasure/Relic system, Economy, Manuals/Techniques

## Core thesis

> Equipment should change how the player solves problems, not merely increase a score.

The equipment system must support three fantasies at once:

1. finding or buying a practical upgrade;
2. mastering different weapon families;
3. keeping a beloved piece of equipment long enough for it to become part of the character's story.

Guild Rank is not an equipment tier.

---

# 1. Five independent equipment axes

Never compress every item into one rarity number.

## A. Base form
What is it physically?

Examples:
- sword;
- spear;
- bow;
- hammer;
- shield;
- coat;
- cuirass;
- ring.

## B. Workmanship
How well was this particular object made?

Working bands:
- Serviceable
- Fine
- Masterwork

These apply mainly to ordinary crafted gear.

**Terminology rule:** `Worn` is reserved for equipment **Condition**, not Workmanship.

## C. Material
What is it made from?

Material affects:
- weight;
- resilience;
- repair behavior;
- environmental suitability;
- Pattern response.

## D. Modification
What has the owner/crafter intentionally changed?

Examples:
- grip;
- edge profile;
- reinforcement;
- socketed component;
- Pattern fitting;
- cosmetic engraving.

## E. Provenance / Relic significance
Does the object have history or unusual world function?

This is governed by the Treasure/Relic system and is NOT a simple combat rarity ladder.

A Serviceable historical tool may be more important to a mystery than a Masterwork sword.

---

# 2. Weapon families

Each family must have a distinct combat language and at least one field/life identity.

| Family | Combat identity | Field identity |
|---|---|---|
| Sword | balanced timing, guard, riposte, adaptable reach | brush/tool cutting, ceremonial/social variants |
| Spear | reach control, thrust, brace, formation | hunting, probing terrain, keeping distance from hazards |
| Axe | committed cleave, hook, momentum | woodcraft, obstacle clearing |
| Hammer/Mace | impact, stagger, armor/tool interaction | repair, breaking damaged structures safely |
| Dagger/Knife | speed, close control, feint, utility | harvesting, crafting, rope/food/tool use |
| Bow | range, aim, preparation, target selection | hunting, signaling, line delivery |
| Crossbow | deliberate powerful shot, reload planning | mechanical specialist tool; final roster validation required |
| Staff | spacing, defense, Pattern support | walking/probing/focus mounting |
| Pattern Focus | casting precision, channel, ward, diagnosis | research, sensing, infrastructure work |
| Shield | brace, cover, deflect, formation | rescue cover, environmental protection |

Do not add a family unless its play pattern is meaningfully different.

---

# 3. Weapon handling, not hard class locks

A player can pick up most ordinary weapons.

Effectiveness comes from:

- familiarity;
- technique knowledge;
- discipline synergy;
- equipment fit;
- player execution.

A Guardian may use a spear.
A Pattern Scholar may carry a sword.
A Hunter may learn dagger techniques.

Specialization should feel valuable without making experimentation impossible.

---

# 4. Weapon geometry matters

Weapon choice changes:

- reach;
- arc;
- windup;
- active window;
- recovery;
- movement commitment;
- stamina/effort;
- guard options;
- target count;
- environmental usability.

This connects directly to the planned combat phases:

> windup -> active -> recovery

A faster weapon does not simply have lower damage.
A long weapon does not simply have higher range.

---

# 5. Armor philosophy

Armor is a trade-off, not a linear Light < Medium < Heavy ladder.

## Flexible clothing / field wear
Strengths:
- mobility;
- quiet movement;
- weather customization;
- low maintenance.

## Reinforced light armor
Strengths:
- mobility plus targeted protection;
- scouts/rangers/general adventurers.

## Medium armor
Strengths:
- balanced protection and endurance;
- broad expedition use.

## Heavy armor
Strengths:
- impact protection;
- formation/frontline stability.

Costs:
- weight;
- heat;
- noise;
- fatigue;
- maintenance;
- some terrain constraints.

## Pattern protective gear
Specialist pieces protecting against:
- heat;
- interference;
- contamination;
- specific environmental hazards.

Not automatically good against physical attacks.

# 6. Equipment slots

Keep the visible system understandable.

Core combat:
- Main Hand
- Off Hand
- Head
- Body
- Hands
- Legs
- Feet

Utility:
- Tool
- Field Kit
- Accessory 1
- Accessory 2

Optional later:
- cloak/back;
- ammunition/quiver;
- Pattern focus mount.

Do not create 20 mandatory stat slots.

---

# 7. Shields

Shields are active tools.

Possible identities:
- buckler — timing/deflection;
- round shield — mobile general defense;
- heater/kite style — directional protection;
- tower/pavise specialist — formation/ranged cover.

Shield mastery can unlock:
- Cover Ally;
- Brace;
- Deflect;
- Bash;
- Rescue Cover.

---

# 8. Pattern equipment

Pattern Magic uses equipment as calibration and stabilization, not infinite mana batteries.

Possible focus forms:
- staff;
- rod;
- lens;
- ring;
- plate;
- instrument;
- crafted tool.

A focus can specialize in:
- Sensing;
- Binding;
- Heat Exchange;
- Impulse;
- Vital Care;
- diagnostics.

Non-mages may carry narrow-use Pattern tools.

---

# 9. Durability and condition

Goal: maintenance creates craft/economic value without making players afraid to use favorite gear.

Use **Condition**, not constant surprise destruction.

Working states:
- Good
- Worn
- Damaged
- Critical

Normal play:
- causes gradual wear;
- repair restores function;
- UI warns before serious penalties.

Defeat/severe environmental damage may create larger condition loss.

Ordinary equipment can eventually become inefficient to restore after repeated catastrophic damage, but:

> Named/Signature/Relic gear must never vanish unexpectedly.

Historical objects use restoration rules rather than disposable durability.

---

# 10. Repair

Repair paths:

## Field maintenance
Small recovery with kits/skill.

## Town repair
Reliable ordinary restoration.

## Specialist restoration
For:
- Masterwork gear;
- unusual materials;
- Pattern fittings;
- relics.

Repair cost depends on:
- damage;
- material;
- complexity;
- replacement parts.

It should not scale merely because the player is high Rank.

---

# 11. Enhancement — reject +1 to +99 treadmill

Do not build a generic enhancement ladder where every weapon becomes:

> Sword +1 -> +2 -> ... -> +99

Problems:
- inflation;
- destroys item identity;
- makes old gear obsolete;
- encourages gambling/failure loops;
- reduces crafting to numerical feeding.

Use **meaningful modifications** instead.

---

# 12. Modification system

A piece has a small number of meaningful modification opportunities.

Examples:

## Edge / head treatment
Changes:
- cutting behavior;
- durability;
- target specialization.

## Grip / balance
Changes:
- recovery;
- control;
- stamina/effort profile.

## Reinforcement
Changes:
- durability;
- weight.

## Pattern fitting
Adds a narrow magical interaction.

## Specialist component
Uses rare material for a specific property.

Modifications should involve trade-offs.

No universal best socket.

Examples from current Oravel content:

## Bellcross Blue Iron
Potential identity:
- reliable;
- resilient;
- repair-friendly.

## Glassroot Fiber
Potential identity:
- light;
- flexible;
- useful in bowstrings/bindings.

## Warmstone
Potential identity:
- expedition/environmental utility.

## Old-World Ceramic
Potential identity:
- heat and Pattern resistance;
- brittle/complex restoration trade-off.

## Starfall Nickel
Potential identity:
- specialist alloy/research use.

# 14. Signature Gear

This is the core long-term ownership system.

An ordinary item can become **Signature Gear** when the player develops enough meaningful history with it.

Not through kill count alone.

Potential recognition signals:
- long-term use;
- important contract completion;
- repairs by known craftspeople;
- learned compatible techniques;
- notable expedition;
- player customization;
- surviving a major event;
- documented provenance.

Signature is identity/status, not an automatic rarity upgrade.

---

# 15. Signature Gear growth tracks

A Signature item can progress horizontally through several tracks.

## Mastery
Owner understands the weapon better.

Unlocks:
- handling refinements;
- technique interactions;
- stance options.

## Craft
Physical improvements/restoration.

Unlocks:
- better fit;
- material replacement;
- specialized components.

## Story
Provenance grows.

Records:
- major contracts;
- Named Monster encounter;
- expeditions;
- maker/restorer;
- important discovery.

## Pattern
If compatible, the object can acquire or reveal narrow Pattern interactions.

## Legacy
At high significance, the object may become a Named item in the world's records.

None requires Rank S.

---

# 16. Example: E-rank sword -> S-rank companion

A player buys a Fine Bellcross sword around Rank E.

### E
The sword is simply good equipment.
Player adjusts grip and learns Returning Step.

### D
A Bellcross smith reforges a damaged guard after a rescue expedition.
The repairer's mark enters provenance.

### C
Player chooses Blue Iron reinforcement rather than replacing the weapon.
It becomes heavier but more resilient.

### B
A compatible manual reveals a precision counter technique.
This is player mastery, not raw weapon level.

### A
The sword survives an important hidden-realm expedition.
Player commissions a Pattern fitting for a narrow diagnostic/defensive use.

### S
Guild archive recognizes the weapon by its accumulated history.
It may receive a player-chosen/name-earned identity.

It is not automatically the strongest sword on the server.

It is **their sword**.

A newly crafted S-context Masterwork sword may have better raw properties.
The old Signature sword may have better familiarity, unique modifications, provenance and technique synergy.

That is a real choice.

---

# 17. Catch-up and experimentation

Signature Gear must not trap a player forever.

Rules:
- changing weapon family remains viable;
- basic proficiency catches up reasonably;
- old Signature Gear can be displayed/stored;
- some knowledge transfers between weapon principles;
- players can maintain multiple meaningful loadouts, but not infinite fully optimized sets without effort.

No permanent character ruin from choosing the "wrong" early weapon.

---

# 18. Crafting identity

Crafted gear records:

- maker;
- workshop;
- material batch where important;
- commissioned owner;
- major reforges;
- restorers.

A great crafter can become socially known because their work remains in circulation.

Potential maker progression:
- reliability;
- specialization;
- signature style;
- commissions;
- restoration skill;
- historical reputation.

Do not make crafting fame equal to producing 10,000 junk swords.

---

# 19. Commission system

Players can request:

- weapon family;
- budget;
- material;
- workmanship target;
- modification;
- cosmetic direction.

Crafter supplies:
- quote;
- material needs;
- expected result range;
- completion conditions.

Escrow protects both sides.

This connects equipment to the cooperative market.

---

# 20. Loot philosophy

Monsters should not routinely drop complete human-made swords from nowhere.

Loot should follow ecology/context.

Examples:
- beast -> biological material;
- bandit -> used equipment;
- ruin -> old components/relics;
- construct -> manufactured components;
- chest/cache -> stored gear.

Most high-quality normal equipment should come from:
- craftspeople;
- institutions;
- believable caches;
- named owners.

---

# 21. Named Monster equipment

Named Monsters may provide materials used to create distinctive equipment.

Rule:

> Named Monster material enables a property; it does not automatically create a legendary weapon.

Example:
Lantern Eater Scale could enable heat/Pattern sensing in a specialist fitting.

This preserves crafting relevance.

---

# 22. Relics versus equipment

Relics can be:

- weapon;
- armor;
- tool;
- key;
- map;
- instrument;
- archive object.

A World Relic weapon is not automatically best-in-slot.

Relic value may come from:
- access;
- knowledge;
- unusual interaction;
- provenance.

---

# 23. Equipment loss and player trust

Default direction:
- no full-loot PvP;
- no random permanent loss of Signature Gear;
- no enhancement failure destroying beloved gear;
- no paid protection consumable needed to avoid destruction.

Risk can target:
- repair cost;
- temporary condition;
- expedition supplies;
- opportunity;
- recovery mission.

Player attachment requires trust.

---

# 24. Trade and binding

Ordinary crafted equipment:
- freely tradeable.

Signature Gear:
- can normally be stored and retired;
- trade rules require design validation because provenance is owner-specific.

Relics:
- follow relic rules.

Avoid arbitrary soulbinding.

Possible provenance-safe trade:
- item remains tradeable;
- previous owner stays in history;
- personal mastery bonuses do not transfer;
- new owner builds their own familiarity.

---

# 25. Cosmetic identity

Visual customization should include:

- material finish;
- grip/wrap;
- scabbard;
- maker mark;
- engraving;
- repair marks;
- expedition ribbons/tokens where culturally appropriate.

Cosmetic history can make two mechanically similar swords feel different.

---

# 26. Equipment and casual play

Noncombat equipment matters too:

- fishing rods;
- cooking tools;
- gardening tools;
- appraisal lenses;
- restoration kits;
- musical instruments;
- cartography tools;
- crafting tools.

These can also develop provenance/signature identity.

A famous fishing rod is valid long-term progression.

---

# 27. Economy safeguards

Equipment system must create sustainable demand through:

- repairs;
- commissions;
- modifications;
- restoration;
- tools;
- Guild requisitions;
- study/disassembly;
- alternate loadouts.

Do not depend on constantly invalidating old equipment.

---

# 28. Prototype scope

For first RPGJS combat validation, implement only:

- Sword
- Spear
- Bow
- Staff/Focus
- Shield if feasible
- 3 armor weight profiles
- Good/Worn/Damaged condition
- one simple modification per weapon
- basic provenance fields

Do NOT implement:
- full Signature progression;
- all materials;
- relic crafting;
- auction integration;
- dozens of sockets.

Validate combat feel first.

---

# 29. Acceptance tests

The equipment design succeeds if:

1. sword/spear/bow/staff feel mechanically distinct;
2. an E-rank weapon can remain emotionally/mechanically relevant later;
3. replacing gear is a choice, not mandatory every few levels;
4. crafters have recurring value;
5. repair is understandable, not punitive;
6. rare materials create specialization, not universal power;
7. no +99/gambling destruction treadmill is required;
8. relics can be valuable without being highest DPS;
9. casual/life-skill equipment can matter;
10. Guild Rank cannot be purchased through gear.

## Open questions

- two-handed versus one-handed rules;
- dual wielding;
- ammunition abstraction;
- armor mitigation formula;
- exact modification slot count;
- Signature recognition thresholds;
- player-to-player transfer behavior for Signature Gear.
