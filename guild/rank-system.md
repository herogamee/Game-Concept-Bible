# Rank and contract access

**Foundation lock:** F → E → D → C → B → A → S; rank is professional trust, not character level.

| Rank | Professional expectation | Ordinary scope | Example evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| F | Registered beginner; safe preparation and truthful reporting | Local deliveries, edge gathering, isolated pests and marked surveys | Completed work, route log, a responsible retreat |
| E | Proven beginner; reliable small-group operation | Nearby escort, small nests, supervised early dungeon | First aid, hazard judgment, basic party agreement |
| D | Professional; can sustain independent field work | Multi-day routes, bandits, ruins, minor expeditions | Supplies managed, contingency executed, verified report |
| C | Veteran; manages a party under uncertainty | Complex investigation, elite threats, village rescue | Coordinated rescue, leadership and evidence quality |
| B | Elite; can coordinate several teams | Regional outbreaks, dangerous expeditions | Logistics and command with civilian accountability |
| A | National-class; sustained responsibility across institutions | National crisis, diplomatic/military charter work | Major achievement and independent recommendations |
| S | Rare legendary qualification | Exceptional catastrophes and deep mysteries | Historic accomplishment and senior scrutiny |

S remains the normal ceiling. Titles recognize achievements without adding SS/SSS inflation. An out-of-ladder calamity classification may describe a threat or person, but is not a new grind ladder.

## Three separate axes

1. Capability: trained skills, health, equipment and knowledge.
2. Rank: verified professional trust and qualification.
3. Standing/endorsements: conduct restrictions, local law, specialist permits.

A powerful F arrival still needs licensing and evidence. An injured C veteran retains rank but may lack current medical clearance for an expedition. A misconduct restriction can remove privileges without deleting strength or every historical achievement. UI explains all three instead of displaying a single number.

## Eligibility algorithm in plain language

Same-rank/lower work is normally available, subject to explicit objective requirements and temporary standing restrictions. For **one-rank-above** work, the branch checks published conditions and issues a named permit. Two or more ranks above is unavailable to that participant. A leader's rank cannot raise the entire party's legal ceiling.

Proposed restricted F → E job conditions: two verified F field reports; no active misconduct restriction; suitable supplies; expected-return registration; and an approved E-or-higher mentor participating in the expedition. Examiner-approved individual exception paths can be designed later; the slice uses this single transparent route. The Guild explains missing conditions before accepting payment or supplies.

Example: F player plus C leader can join an **E** job if approved, but cannot join a **D** job. E player can request D work under its posted restrictions. S has no above-rank letter; extraordinary S contracts add scope/endorsement requirements instead.

## Risk and information

Advertised rank is estimated operational demand: threat, distance, terrain, time, information uncertainty and civilian responsibility. It is not the highest enemy's numeric level. Confidence labels from v0.1 remain Verified, Reliable, Incomplete, Rumor, Emergency and Classified. Emergency and Classified describe handling context rather than a mathematically comparable certainty score; underlying report certainty is still recorded.

If a legitimate F job reveals a D threat, the player may retreat, mark the location and return for reassessment. No retroactive D permit is implied. An emergency supply task near that threat may still be F if the marked safe route stays outside combat exposure.

Rank gating should explain contractual trust, not replace physical warnings or readable combat. Players may explore uncontracted routes where law permits, but the slice signals danger and permits retreat. Forced impossible encounters are not an examination of courage.
