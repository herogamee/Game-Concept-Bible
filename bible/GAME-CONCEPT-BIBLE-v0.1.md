# GAME CONCEPT BIBLE v0.1 — Foundation

**Working genre:** Adventurer Life RPG  
**Status:** Pre-production concept  
**Canonical repository:** herogamee/Game-Concept-Bible  
**Date:** 2026-10-01

---

## 1. One-sentence pitch

A fantasy RPG where the player begins as an unknown **F-rank adventurer**, joins a living Adventurer Guild, takes ranked contracts, builds skills and relationships, travels between towns and villages, and gradually earns the right to challenge a mysterious dungeon descending 100 floors beneath the world.

---

## 2. Player fantasy

The player is **not** the chosen hero at the beginning.

The desired feeling is:

- I arrived with almost nothing.
- My first weapon was cheap.
- Goblins were genuinely dangerous.
- I had to choose which jobs were worth the risk.
- I met other new adventurers who had ambitions of their own.
- The guild receptionist eventually learned my name.
- Villagers began recognizing me.
- My first promotion felt earned.
- I remember the people I lost or left behind.
- Years later, walking into the same guild as an A- or S-rank adventurer should feel completely different.

The emotional arc is:

> **Nobody → Survivor → Professional → Veteran → Renowned Adventurer → Living Legend**

The game should make the player remember the journey, not only the final stats.

---

## 3. Core design pillars

### 3.1 Start from zero

F-rank must not be a tutorial rank that disappears in 20 minutes.

Early game should include weak equipment, limited money, basic lodging, small jobs, local maps, incomplete monster knowledge, real danger from low-tier enemies, and reasons to prepare before leaving town.

The first goblin group should matter.

### 3.2 The Guild is a place, not a menu

The Adventurer Guild is the player's professional home.

It contains registration, quest board, tavern, party recruitment, appraisal, training, promotion exams, records, bulletin board, emergency systems, and NPC adventurers with schedules and relationships.

At F rank, nobody notices the player. At A rank, new adventurers may whisper when the player enters.

### 3.3 Guild Rank is not Character Level

Character progression measures personal capability: level or equivalent growth, attributes, skills, class mastery, equipment, and knowledge.

Guild Rank measures professional trust: completed contracts, difficulty handled, reliability, conduct, leadership, discoveries, public reputation, and examinations.

A powerful new arrival can still be low guild rank. A high-rank adventurer can lose privileges through misconduct.

### 3.4 A living world

NPC adventurers can take quests, form and split parties, fail jobs, get injured, retire, rise in rank, become famous, become rivals, open businesses, disappear, or die if permanent NPC death is later approved.

A player may meet an F-rank mage on Day 3 and find her much later as a C-rank party leader, injured veteran, noble retainer, missing person, or someone who never advanced.

### 3.5 Exploration contains uncertainty

Quest information can be wrong because the client estimated poorly, monsters migrated, weather changed, another faction interfered, the dungeon altered, or the requester concealed information.

The target story is: “That was supposed to be a D-rank wolf hunt. Why was an ogre there?”

### 3.6 The 100-floor dungeon is a world mystery

The dungeon is important but not the whole world. The surface contains cities, villages, roads, forests, ruins, caves, mines, temples, faction territories, borders, and smaller dungeons.

## 4. Core gameplay loop

### Daily loop

1. Wake at inn, home, or camp.
2. Check health, gear, supplies, weather.
3. Visit guild or contacts.
4. Review contracts and rumors.
5. Choose risk versus reward.
6. Recruit or meet companions.
7. Buy, repair, craft, prepare.
8. Travel.
9. Explore, investigate, negotiate, fight, or gather.
10. React to changing conditions.
11. Return and report.
12. Receive money, guild merit, reputation.
13. Sell materials, repair, rest.
14. Socialize, train, research, pursue goals.
15. Advance world simulation.

### Long-term loop

**Take jobs → gain experience → build relationships → improve equipment → gain guild merit → pass promotions → access new regions and contracts → discover larger threats → descend deeper → influence the world**

---

## 5. Adventurer Guild Rank

Main ladder:

**F → E → D → C → B → A → S**

S is the normal ceiling. The design avoids automatic SS/SSS inflation.

Exceptional S-rank adventurers earn titles or special classifications such as:

- S Rank — Dragon Slayer
- S Rank — Abyss Walker
- S Rank — Saint of the Northern Road
- S Rank — Last Vanguard
- S Rank — Floorbreaker

Possible world-level classifications can exist outside the normal ladder, such as Mythic, Heroic, Calamity, Transcendent, or National Asset.

### Rank roles

**F — Registered Beginner:** basic local work, gathering, delivery, pests, scouting, isolated low-tier monsters.

**E — Proven Beginner:** small monster groups, nearby escorts, beginner caves, low-risk resource zones.

**D — Professional Adventurer:** dangerous beasts, bandits, ruins, longer routes, minor dungeon expeditions.

**C — Veteran:** large nests, elite enemies, village emergencies, specialist investigations, leadership.

**B — Elite:** outbreaks, noble contracts, dangerous dungeon operations, regional defense.

**A — National-Class:** national crises, dragon-level threats, deep expeditions, diplomatic and military work.

**S — Legendary:** rare assignments involving cities, nations, ancient monsters, catastrophic dungeon events, or world secrets.

---

## 6. Quest eligibility

Base rule:

> Adventurers freely accept quests at their own rank or below and may attempt **one rank above** under restrictions.

A D-rank adventurer normally accepts F/E/D. A C-rank job may require guild permission, party conditions, a risk waiver, reputation, a C-rank leader, or special circumstances.

Higher-rank contracts should be tempting enough that players sometimes knowingly take a bad risk.

---

## 7. Promotion system

Promotion is not automatic XP.

Requirements combine completed contracts, guild merit, reliability, capability, conduct, discoveries, and examinations.

- **F → E:** basic navigation, monster ID, combat, first aid, survival.
- **E → D:** multi-day field test with limited guild assistance.
- **D → C:** party operation, rescue, boss hunt, investigation, judgment.
- **C → B:** leadership test commanding lower-rank adventurers.
- **B → A:** major achievements, recommendations, difficult expedition.
- **A → S:** historic accomplishment and senior approval rather than grind.

---

## 8. Professions and classes

### Starting archetypes

1. Fighter
2. Ranger
3. Mage
4. Rogue
5. Cleric
6. Scout

### Example evolution

**Fighter:** Knight, Guardian, Berserker, Swordmaster, Duelist  
**Ranger:** Hunter, Marksman, Beastmaster, Pathfinder  
**Mage:** Elementalist, Summoner, Arcane Scholar, Battle Mage, hidden forbidden paths  
**Rogue:** Assassin, Treasure Hunter, Shadow, Saboteur  
**Cleric:** Priest, Paladin, Oracle, Exorcist  
**Scout:** Explorer, Tracker, Cartographer, Monster Scholar

Advanced classes should be discovered through mentors, books, factions, hidden quests, achievements, unusual equipment, world events, and player behavior rather than being fully visible from the first menu.

---

## 9. Party design

Expected standard party size: **1–4** initially.

Contributions can include frontline, ranged damage, healing, shielding, scouting, tracking, lockpicking, appraisal, negotiation, cooking, mapping, monster knowledge, gathering, and magical utility.

NPC companions can trust or distrust the player, refuse reckless missions, demand higher pay, leave, become permanent allies, or become independent rivals.

---

## 10. Quest system

Foundation quest families:

1. Hunt
2. Gather
3. Escort
4. Explore
5. Rescue / Search
6. Investigation

These can combine and transform.

A Gather quest may become an Investigation after poisoned soil is discovered.

Contracts also have information confidence:
- Verified
- Reliable
- Incomplete
- Rumor
- Emergency
- Classified

Hidden quests may trigger through rumors, environmental clues, timing, unusual items, previous decisions, or relationships. Not every important event needs a map marker.

---

## 11. World structure

The world expands with professional status.

**F–E:** starter city, farms, 2–3 villages, roads, forest, beginner cave, first dungeon entrance.  
**D–C:** wider region, towns, dangerous roads, mines, ruins, faction outposts, regional politics.  
**B:** capital, noble territories, military conflicts, major guild branches.  
**A:** other countries and frontier territories.  
**S:** forbidden regions, lost civilizations, world-scale dungeon phenomena, international crises.

The map should feel as if it grows alongside the player's reputation.

---

## 12. Settlements

Every settlement needs an economic and gameplay purpose.

Examples:

- Starter City — major guild branch near the 100-floor dungeon.
- Farming Village — food production and low-rank monster trouble.
- Mining Town — ore, blacksmithing, underground threats.
- River Trade Town — merchants, escorts, smuggling.
- Mountain Village — herbs, weather, migrations.
- Mage City — academies, magical licensing, arcane quests.
- Border Fort — military contracts and faction tension.
- Port City — sea travel, islands, underwater content.

Settlements should change because of events and player choices.

---

## 13. The 100-floor dungeon

Current structure: **10 strata × 10 floors**

### Floors 1–10 — Beginner Depths
Caves, slime, goblins, cave wolves, beginner resources.

### Floors 11–20 — Ancient Mines
Kobolds, orcs, abandoned machinery, ore economy.

### Floors 21–30 — Underground Wilds
Forest ecosystem, giant insects, predators, hidden settlements.

### Floors 31–40 — Sunken City
Ruins, water hazards, undead, lost records.

### Floors 41–50 — Demon Ruins
Corrupted architecture, intelligent hostile factions, stronger magic.

### Floors 51–60 — Crystal Abyss
Unstable magic, rare resources, environmental danger.

### Floors 61–70 — Dragon Graveyard
Ancient remains, apex predators, relic hunters.

### Floors 71–80 — Lost Kingdom
Evidence of a civilization that should not be there.

### Floors 81–90 — Abyss
Normal rules begin to fail.

### Floors 91–99 — Unknown
Official guild maps are incomplete or unreliable.

### Floor 100 — Classified
The truth is intentionally undefined in v0.1 and must become a core narrative mystery.

Every tenth floor may provide a major boss, sanctuary, logistical unlock, revelation, or expedition checkpoint, but the pattern should not become mechanically predictable.

## 14. Dungeon ecology

The dungeon should behave like an ecosystem.

Design questions:
- What do monsters eat?
- Why do species inhabit specific floors?
- What happens when predators migrate?
- Who harvests resources?
- Can merchants operate on safe floors?
- Can monsters leave?
- Can species be overhunted?
- Can factions live inside?
- Does the dungeon regenerate?
- Is the dungeon alive?
- Why exactly 100 floors?

These become later systems, not cosmetic lore.

## 15. NPC Adventurer Simulation

This is a potential signature system.

Important adventurer NPC data may include:

- name;
- age;
- profession;
- guild rank;
- capability;
- traits;
- risk tolerance;
- party;
- relationships;
- money;
- equipment quality;
- injuries;
- ambitions;
- current job;
- reputation;
- location.

Simulation tiers:

**Tier A — Major NPC:** high-fidelity simulation and authored arcs.  
**Tier B — Persistent Adventurer:** career and relationship simulation.  
**Tier C — Background Adventurer:** lightweight statistical simulation.

This creates a living guild without simulating every person at maximum fidelity.

---

## 16. Reputation

Reputation is multi-dimensional rather than one Good/Evil bar.

Tracks can include:
- Adventurer Guild;
- towns and villages;
- merchants;
- nobles;
- military;
- religious factions;
- criminal groups;
- individual NPCs.

A player may be loved by villagers, distrusted by nobles, famous among adventurers, and hated by smugglers simultaneously.

---

## 17. Economy

Money should matter, especially early.

Early expenses:
- inn;
- food;
- healing;
- repair;
- ammunition;
- potions;
- guild fees;
- transport;
- maps;
- party shares.

Later expenses can expand into rare equipment, crafting, housing, mounts, expeditions, hired staff, and faction investment.

Avoid an economy where low denominations become meaningless after two hours.

---

## 18. Equipment philosophy

Equipment should have identity.

A weapon can matter because the player saved for it, a known blacksmith made it, it survived major battles, it has been repaired repeatedly, or it gained a name/property.

Possible quality framework:
- Common
- Fine
- Rare
- Masterwork
- Relic
- Legendary

Final terminology is open.

---

## 19. Failure

Failure should often create a story instead of always forcing reload.

Possible outcomes:
- injury;
- lost equipment;
- reduced reward;
- reputation damage;
- failed client objective;
- relationship damage;
- rescue contract generated;
- region changes because the quest failed.

Player death rules are not decided in v0.1. NPC permanent consequences remain under consideration.

---

## 20. Dynamic world events

Examples:
- monster migration;
- goblin settlement growth;
- caravan shortage;
- plague;
- war;
- flood;
- drought;
- dungeon overflow;
- guild expedition;
- festival;
- noble succession;
- bandit activity;
- ruin discovery.

Events affect quests, travel, prices, and NPC activity. The quest board becomes a window into world simulation.

## 21. First-hour prototype experience

### Minute 0–10 — Arrival

The player reaches the starter city with basic clothes, little money, and no fame.

The city establishes the guild, dungeon landmark, market, inn, and ordinary people living around the adventurer economy.

### Minute 10–20 — Guild Registration

The player registers, chooses a starting aptitude/class direction, receives an F-rank tag, hears the liability rules, and sees the quest board.

The board deliberately contains jobs the player cannot yet accept. The player sees the future.

### Minute 20–30 — First choice

Example F-rank contracts:

1. Gather medicinal herbs outside the east gate.
2. Remove slimes from an irrigation canal.
3. Deliver a sealed package to a nearby farm.
4. Find a missing goat.
5. Join another beginner for goblin scouting.

No mandatory correct first quest.

### Minute 30–45 — Field work

The player learns navigation, stamina, basic combat, gathering, and risk.

A simple quest may contain one unexpected event.

Example: missing-goat tracks become wolf tracks. The player can retreat, investigate, or seek help.

### Minute 45–60 — Return

The player reports, receives the first reward and guild merit, sees other adventurers return from their own jobs, hears a dungeon rumor, and notices a high-rank adventurer enter.

Target feeling:

> “I am tiny in this world, but I want to become one of them.”

## 22. Inspiration study map

These are design references, not content to copy.

### Japanese / anime / manga study

- **Goblin Slayer** — grounded adventurer work, guild atmosphere, low-tier danger.
- **DanMachi** — city shaped around a great dungeon, adventurer progression.
- **KonoSuba** — guild as social space and daily life.
- **Grimgar** — vulnerability, equipment value, party dependence.
- **Frieren** — qualifications, exams, exploration, history.
- **Solo Leveling** — readable rank language and power contrast.
- **Sword Art Online / Aincrad** — fascination of numbered floors.
- **Mushoku Tensei** — travel, culture, language, world scale.
- **Re:Zero** — consequences and memorable NPC relationships.
- **That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime** — factions and settlement development.
- **Overlord** — adventurer classification and world power hierarchy.
- **Shangri-La Frontier** — hidden conditions and player-discovered scenarios.
- **Dungeon Meshi** — dungeon ecology and resources.
- **Made in Abyss** — emotional power of descending into the unknown.

### Chinese fantasy / donghua study

Study long-term progression, sect/faction identity, crafting/alchemy, rare resources, dangerous realms, and milestone-based power growth.

Potential works:
- A Record of a Mortal's Journey to Immortality
- Battle Through the Heavens
- Soul Land
- Renegade Immortal

The final IP must use original names, lore, factions, art, characters, and mechanic combinations.

## 23. Market positioning hypothesis

Working positioning:

> **“The fantasy RPG for players who want to live the adventurer life they see in anime, not just watch it.”**

The project should differentiate through the combination of:

1. living Adventurer Guild;
2. meaningful F→S professional progression;
3. persistent NPC adventurer careers;
4. quest uncertainty;
5. world-state consequences;
6. 100-floor mystery;
7. fantasy life simulation between battles.

---

## 24. Multiplayer direction — not decided

Possible paths:

**A — Single-player first:** richest NPC simulation and authored world.  
**B — Co-op 1–4:** real player parties while NPC society remains important.  
**C — Shared-world online:** many adventurers share guilds and economy.

v0.1 does not commit to MMO architecture. A strong single-player/co-op core is the safer prototype basis.

---

## 25. Combat direction — open

Candidates:
- real-time action RPG;
- tactical real-time;
- action with party commands;
- turn-based tactical.

The choice must support readable party roles, solo viability, meaningful preparation, target platform constraints, and manageable development scope.

Prototype feel before designing hundreds of skills.

---

## 26. Art direction — open

Desired high-level feel:
- readable anime-inspired fantasy;
- warm, lively towns;
- dangerous wilderness;
- increasingly surreal deep dungeon;
- original silhouettes and world identity;
- strong character customization.

The player should feel this is **their adventurer**.

---

## 27. Scope guardrails

Do not build all 100 floors first.

First vertical slice should contain roughly:

- 1 starter city;
- 1 guild;
- 1 village;
- 1 road/wilderness;
- 1 small cave;
- dungeon floors 1–3 or equivalent;
- F and E ranks;
- 3–4 starting classes;
- 10–20 quests;
- 10–20 persistent adventurer NPCs;
- one promotion test.

If this slice is not fun, 100 floors will not fix it.

## 29. Current decisions

### Locked for foundation

- Player is an adventurer, not primarily a guild master.
- Player begins at F rank.
- Main ladder is F → E → D → C → B → A → S.
- Player may attempt up to one rank above under restrictions.
- Guild rank is separate from character power.
- Adventurer Guild is a physical social hub.
- Cities and villages exist beyond the main dungeon.
- Main dungeon targets 100 floors.
- Floors are grouped into larger strata.
- NPC adventurers progress independently.
- Hidden quests and discovery matter.
- Original IP is mandatory.

### Open

- final game title;
- world name;
- exact combat model;
- single-player vs co-op vs shared online;
- death rules;
- races;
- magic system;
- final classes;
- authored/procedural dungeon balance;
- commercial model;
- platforms.

---

## 30. North-star question

Whenever a feature is proposed, ask:

> **“Does this make me feel more like I am actually living the life of an adventurer in a world that continues without me?”**

If not, it needs justification.
