# GAME CONCEPT BIBLE v0.2 — World, Guild, Factions & Starter Region

**Date:** 2026-10-01 | **Status:** Design proposal; checkpoint 2 of 3  
**Predecessor:** [v0.1, preserved](GAME-CONCEPT-BIBLE-v0.1.md)  
**Audience:** designers, writers, programmers, artists, producers and playtesters.

## 1. What game are we making?

An original Adventurer Life RPG: an ordinary person registers at F rank, makes a living through dangerous contracts, forms relationships, earns professional trust and explores a world that keeps moving. The emotional progression remains Nobody → Survivor → Professional → Veteran → Renowned Adventurer → Living Legend.

The player controls an adventurer. Guild administration supports their life; it is not the main player occupation. The first prototype proves a short journey from anonymous applicant to recognized E-rank professional, rather than building an entire kingdom or 100 floors.

## 2. Authority and decision status

v0.1 §29 locks remain unchanged: adventurer identity; F start; F → E → D → C → B → A → S; restricted access to one rank above; rank separate from power; physical Guild; surface settlements; approximately 100 floors in strata; independent NPC careers; discovery; Original IP.

Everything new in v0.2 is a **working proposal**, not a newly owner-approved lock. Working names support coherent writing and may change. Numerical prices, promotion thresholds and pacing are **prototype tuning assumptions**. No commercial, combat, platform, permanent-death or multiplayer decision is silently resolved here.

Source precedence: foundation locks → explicit recorded owner decisions → v0.2 overview → linked specialist documents for detail. Research describes other works or community opinion; it does not override our design. When documents conflict, record and resolve the conflict instead of selecting whichever is convenient.

## 3. The world in one page

Working world name **Oravel**, starter kingdom **Veyr**, starter region **the Reedmark**, starter city **Ternhaven**. These are original working labels, pending naming clearance. Two additional world candidates are **Edrava** and **Narethil**; see [world foundation](../world/world-overview.md).

Long ago, a network of underground load-bearing magical works failed. Surface societies recovered without fully understanding the buried systems. In the Reedmark, river levels, mine ventilation and dungeon migrations are beginning to change together. The player initially sees spoiled deliveries and unsafe roads, not a destiny to save the world. Whether the old network caused the main dungeon remains unknown.

The Guild exists because armies defend borders, guards police settlements, and neither can maintain every road, inspect every ruin or organize every rescue. It pools maps, contract escrow, training and professional accountability across borders. Its neutrality is negotiated and imperfect.

## 4. Starter geography and play

Ternhaven links farms, river traffic, metalworking and the main dungeon entrance. Three villages—Aldermead, Fenwick and Highmere—and the secondary town Bellcross support food, ferries, medicine, ore and trade. Brackenwood, the Greyfold mountains, the Sere river, Lantern Mine and Siltwell Cavern make surface adventuring economically necessary.

[Starter region](../world/starter-region.md) owns settlement purpose, connections, travel assumptions, danger boundaries and changing-state hooks. Only Ternhaven, Aldermead, a wilderness route, Siltwell and main floors 1–3 are playable in the first slice. Other locations can appear as map names, goods and correspondence.

## 5. Guild, rank and trust

[Guild operations](../guild/adventurer-guild.md) defines authority, staff, physical rooms, license, contracts, emergency escalation, disputes and politics. [Rank rules](../guild/rank-system.md) keeps qualification separate from combat capability. [Promotion](../guild/promotion-system.md) defines evidence, a practical F → E examination and retakes.

A contract's advertised rank measures expected operational risk, not monster level. Discovering a higher threat lets the player retreat and submit evidence without automatically being branded unreliable. F adventurers can request restricted E work; they cannot take D work through a high-rank party leader loophole.

## 6. Factions, peoples and magic

[Factions](../world/factions.md) describes ten groups with competing interests, asymmetric alliances and jobs for an ordinary adventurer. [Species](../world/species.md) proposes three peoples with social and economic roles; human-only presentation is a slice assumption, not a final exclusion.

[Magic](../world/magic-system.md) proposes bounded conversion of ambient power through trained patterns, bodily effort and consumable materials. It cannot create unlimited food, heal every injury or trivialize transportation. Faith provides institutions and traditions; the objective existence of gods remains open.

## 7. Economy and early vulnerability

[Economy](../world/economy.md) uses copper accounting, published fees, party splitting, salvage rules and worked budgets. A prepared F solo day should provide a small positive surplus. A failed trip hurts, but recovery work and starter equipment loans prevent forced grinding or insolvency. Numbers describe test targets, not a simulated national economy.

## 8. People and an independently changing Guild

[Starter cast](../characters/starter-npcs.md) contains 28 original working characters: 14 support/civic people and 14 persistent adventurers. Each has a goal, relationship, private tension and possible development. The player can encounter a rival, an injured professional, a novice saving for a blade and someone choosing a clinic career. No permanent NPC death is approved.

[NPC simulation](../design/npc-simulation.md) uses discrete saved action ticks, protected player reservations, single settlement and authored outcomes. Visible departures, changed equipment, recovery and news expose change without requiring a global simulation.

## 9. First-ten-hour progression

[First ten hours](../design/first-10-hours.md) preserves the v0.1 first hour and proposes several paths: civic investigation, surveying or social party building. Saving may produce a first permanent weapon around hours 2–4; a permitted small-dungeon trip around hours 3–5; E readiness around hours 7–10. These are test hypotheses, not mandatory time gates.

The [18-contract catalogue](../design/starter-quests.md) covers all six foundation families. There is an affordable recovery job, truthful retreat, environmental clues and explicit party payouts. A floor-3 expedition is later slice content, not a prerequisite for the beginner examination.

## 10. What the team builds first

[Vertical slice](../design/vertical-slice.md): one city district and Guild, one village, one wilderness route, Siltwell's two chambers, main floors 1–3, F/E player ranks, four starting aptitudes, 18 contract templates, 14 persistent adventurers, two regional event chains and one promotion test. Other countries and settlements are future context.

An offline solo test harness with NPC parties is a temporary production assumption. Final combat, networking, platforms, commercial model and death policy remain open. Measure profession comprehension, net-pay comprehension, observed NPC change, recovery from failure and more than one viable route to promotion before enlarging scope.

## 11. Integration checkpoints

Checkpoint 1 established world, factions, magic, economy and Guild rules. Checkpoint 2 integrates 28 NPCs, authored contracts, independent careers, first-ten-hour routes and bounded production scope. Checkpoint 3 will integrate dated market evidence, community samples, reference analysis, decision register and verification.

The final v0.2 must let a developer answer: where does the player begin, why does this place exist, how does the Guild constrain risk, which NPCs change, what earns a promotion, and what exactly is built first?
