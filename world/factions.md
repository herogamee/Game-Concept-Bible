## Institutional distinction

The Guild brokers accountable work; Grey Mantle sells armed time. The Collegium teaches and certifies magical technique; Brass Survey studies archaeological infrastructure. Hearth provides care and rites; it does not automatically control all healing magic. River Charter represents commercial firms; House Talven represents property and inherited authority. Avoid ten factions performing the same “collect relic” function with different costumes.

## Starting conflict: the canal and the cargo

1. Aldermead reports blocked irrigation; a safe F investigation begins.
2. A survey finds mine sediment plus a displaced cave nest, leaving cause uncertain.
3. River Charter asks to reopen freight quickly; Reedkin witnesses warn downstream habitat will suffer.
4. Brass Survey proposes staged opening with measurements. It takes longer and costs wages.
5. House Talven's steward offers a reward for a report blaming animals alone.

Possible player outcomes: publish incomplete evidence honestly; collect a second sample; accept misleading payment; seek help to open a limited channel; prioritize immediate civilian relief. Evidence and consent matter. Avoid a single binary faction bar or making every compromise evil.

In the slice, only Guild, Hearth, River Charter and Brass Survey need full interaction. Reedkin and House Talven appear through one representative and correspondence. Other factions are records and future hooks; no faction-war simulation is required.

## Reputation and conflict rules

Store local standing, institutional trust and individual relationships separately. A merchant may dislike a delay while acknowledging honest testimony. A criminal contact can trust the player without the Guild immediately knowing why. Faction information travels through witnessed events and reports, not omniscient global updates.

Proposed range is -100 to +100 per relevant track, with qualitative UI labels; initial values are 0. Thresholds require playtesting. A single beginner choice should not permanently block an entire career. Consequences can alter prices, offer timing or companion willingness; basic registration, recovery jobs and reporting safety hazards remain available.

NPCs have beliefs, not faction mind control. A guard can disagree with a noble warrant; a Reedkin fisher can support limited engineering works. Do not designate a playable species as inherently criminal or hostile.

## Future quest hooks

- Guild versus Grey Mantle: a rescue retainer forbids diversion to civilians. Find a lawful shared response.
- Hearth versus Collegium: a patient needs an unlicensed treatment; risk, scarcity and informed consent conflict.
- Brass Survey versus Talven: ownership of a ruin does not settle whether removing it threatens water supply.
- River Charter versus Closed Ledger: recover a manifest without exposing an unrelated debtor's private information.
- Crown versus Guild: a border order conflicts with a neutral rescue charter. A higher-rank arc, not an F-rank diplomatic mandate.
