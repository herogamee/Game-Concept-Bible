# World foundation

**Status:** v0.2 working proposal. Names and cosmology remain open.  
**Design purpose:** an inhabited world in which adventuring is a profession, not an excuse for universal warfare.

## Naming candidates

| Candidate | Desired tone | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oravel | Warm surface, uncertain depths | Short enough for dialogue and map labels | Pronunciation needs testing in Thai/Japanese/Chinese |
| Edrava | Older, weathered world | Distinct consonants and restrained fantasy tone | Could sound like a kingdom rather than a world |
| Narethil | More mythic | Fits distant scholarship and long history | Harder to remember; conventional fantasy cadence |

Use **Oravel** in these drafts to avoid placeholders everywhere. None is approved as final or cleared as a trademark. Search and localization clearance precede public branding. Working names below are inventions, not adaptations of reference-world nouns.

## Geography

Three broad landmasses establish future travel without requiring a global playable map:

| Landmass | Climate and economy | Political units | Possible later play |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roven | Temperate river valleys, upland ore, western storms | Veyr kingdom, Halden league of river republics, Karst March compact | Starter travel, rival branches, borders and migrant monsters |
| Istrane | Dry inland plateau and monsoon coasts | Saruun port federation and inland charter cities | Water rights, long caravans, specialist ruins |
| Ulreth | Cold coasts, short growing seasons, fissured mountains | Independent coastal councils | Supply planning, rescue expeditions, relic disputes |

These are coarse regions, not new production commitments. Mountain passes and sea lanes keep countries connected; magic does not replace boats, grain or roads. Guild branches operate by local charter, not universal sovereign power.

**Veyr** has a crown, provincial courts and towns with bargaining rights. A land tax funds border forces; towns fund guards and road works; Guild escrow funds contracted field work. Nobles own some land and mining concessions, but contracts and appeal rights limit arbitrary seizure. Rights can fail in practice, creating conflicts rather than a universally benevolent monarchy.

The Reedmark lies downstream of the Greyfold watershed. Its river supports food, freight and mills. Ternhaven sits beside a stone shelf above the floodplain, near a deep entrance people call **the Underfold**. Bellcross controls the northern ore road. The capital is **Crownreach**, outside slice scope. Do not equate being higher rank with a literal invisible border: difficult logistics and legal contract access explain the expanding map.

## Historical layers

| Relative date | Publicly recorded event | Present consequence |
|---|---|---|
| About 430 years ago | Collapse of several buried pressure works; survivors disagree on cause | Unstable underground water and lost surveys |
| About 300 years ago | River towns reopen roads and mining routes | Local families retain competing salvage claims |
| About 190 years ago | Uncoordinated recovery parties lose many workers in a major flood | Demand for shared maps, rescue signals and escrow |
| About 184 years ago | First inter-town Adventurer Compact | Predecessor of today's Guild licenses |
| About 96 years ago | Veyr recognizes traveling Guild credentials | Guild can operate across provincial borders |
| About 27 years ago | A deep expedition returns with inconsistent measurements | Archive disputes whether floor counts are stable |
| Present campaign | River sediment, mine airflow and lower-floor animal movement correlate | Early contracts become evidence of a regional problem |

Recorded dates are fictional working history. The official expedition catalogue targets **100 floors** in **10 strata**; no character has to possess a trustworthy complete map. v0.1's floor-100 truth remains undefined. Do not reveal the dungeon's origin through a beginner quest or retroactively establish a chosen-one bloodline.

## Knowledge and mystery

Common belief: the Underfold was dug by a vanished civilization and holds dangerous wealth. Religious tradition: the buried works were a duty abandoned by earlier generations. Guild evidence: some pressure gates remain artificial; ecosystem boundaries and floor measurements disagree. These are in-world beliefs and records, not competing author declarations of truth.

Three hypothesis families remain open: failed infrastructure, an altered natural/magical ecosystem, or an intentional containment project. A later narrative decision should explain all observed clues. Avoid pretending that all three are simultaneously established facts.

## Threat ladder

1. Local: missing livestock, spore exposure, washed-out crossings, misidentified predators.
2. Regional: trade interruption, coordinated smuggling, mine displacement, dungeon overflow.
3. National: scarcity politics, contested resource rights, mobilization during floods.
4. World-scale: instability connecting buried systems; exact cause and antagonist remain open.

Surface quests must matter even if the dungeon plot is ignored. An adventurer can preserve a harvest or reunite a family without unlocking an ancient secret.

## Daily culture, technology and faith

Working baseline: mills, carts, hand presses for standardized forms, navigational instruments and skilled steelwork. No industrial mass production, instant long-distance messaging or effortless teleportation. Magic tools are expensive to calibrate and need physical replacement materials. This produces demand for couriers, surveyors, escorts and craftspeople.

Working calendar: 12 months of 30 days plus five civic observance days; the campaign starts near the end of the wet season in year 318 of the Crown Ledger. Regional calendars may differ, so Guild contracts use an explicit day/hour ledger. Calendar lore does not imply a real-time deadline while the player is paused.

The **Hearth Covenant** maintains clinics, public kitchens and burial rites. It teaches stewardship and witness to promises. Veyr permits other local practices; religious dissent is not automatically villainy. Whether prayer works through gods, shared magical practice or both is an open narrative decision.

## Why adventurers, rather than armies?

Armies organize sustained military force but cannot cheaply field many independent specialists on scattered civilian work. Guards operate under settlement jurisdiction. Private mercenaries can escort a convoy but rarely publish hazard reports or maintain a cross-border rescue fund. The Guild supplies training, records, professional trust and an appeal process.

This advantage has costs: fees, slow approvals, biased clients and pressure from patrons. Guild neutrality fails when funding and local law conflict. The player experiences that through a refused job, changed permit or contested report; the design does not turn them into a policy manager.
