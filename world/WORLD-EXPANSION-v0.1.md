# World Expansion v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-02  
**Status:** World-building working proposal  
**Depends on:** `world/world-overview.md`, `world/starter-region.md`

## Purpose

Expand the world beyond the Reedmark without committing production to a giant map before the starter loop works.

The world should feel larger than the player from Rank F onward, but become progressively more reachable as professional trust, contacts, wealth and knowledge grow.

---

# 1. World scale

Working world name: **Oravel**.

Three known major landmasses remain:

- **Roven** — temperate rivers, uplands, established kingdoms and trade leagues.
- **Istrane** — dry inland plateau, monsoon coasts, port federations and caravan cities.
- **Ulreth** — cold coasts, short growing seasons, fissured mountains and independent councils.

These are broad cultural/economic regions, not single-biome continents.

The known world also includes:

- islands;
- offshore ruins;
- interior wilderness;
- disputed frontiers;
- old roads;
- inactive buried-network sites;
- regions absent from reliable modern charts.

---

# 2. Map philosophy

The player should not receive the full world as an unlocked checklist.

Access grows through:

- Guild rank;
- letters of introduction;
- faction trust;
- permits;
- ship routes;
- caravan connections;
- discovered paths;
- political events;
- hidden routes;
- personal contacts.

A high-level map can show names before those places become playable.

The feeling should be:

> "I knew this place existed long before I finally earned a reason to go there."

---

# 3. Settlement design rule

Every important settlement needs:

1. an economic reason to exist;
2. a social identity;
3. a dependency on another place;
4. at least one local problem unrelated to the Main Dungeon;
5. at least one way player actions can visibly change it;
6. a reason to revisit after its first quest line.

Do not build towns only as shops between combat zones.

# 5. Istrane

Tone: distance, heat, water, monsoon, old routes and specialist scholarship.

Potential locations:

## Saruun Port Federation
Coastal city network rather than one centralized kingdom.

Gameplay:
- sea travel;
- guild contracts across ports;
- merchant houses;
- diving/salvage;
- storms;
- island expeditions;
- foreign auction houses.

## Inland Charter Cities
Independent centers built around wells, caravan routes and protected archives.

Gameplay:
- long-range preparation;
- water rights;
- escort;
- ancient road systems;
- desert anomalies;
- rare materials;
- hidden sealed sites.

# 6. Ulreth

Tone: cold coast, dangerous weather, sparse infrastructure and old fissures.

Potential gameplay:

- expedition logistics;
- rescue;
- seasonal routes;
- monster migrations;
- relic disputes;
- deep caves;
- isolated communities;
- maritime hunting;
- ancient inactive network nodes.

High-rank travel here should make preparation and reputation matter.

---

# 7. Unknown / forbidden regions

These are primarily post-A/S content.

Examples:

- a sea region where compasses drift;
- a buried city not connected to known roads;
- an island appearing only during certain tides;
- a sealed mountain basin;
- a zone removed from Guild maps after a failed expedition;
- a region whose distance measurements change;
- a subterranean route connecting distant landmasses.

These places must have discoverable clues.

Do not make them random "secret zones" with no world logic.

---

# 8. Main Dungeon relationship

The Underfold under Ternhaven is one major site, not the whole setting.

Later evidence may reveal:

- sister structures;
- control nodes;
- old maintenance roads;
- sealed junctions;
- failed regional systems.

Surface societies must retain independent histories, cultures and conflicts.

---

# 9. Rank and geographic expansion

Suggested narrative access pattern:

| Rank | Typical geographic scope |
|---|---|
| F | starter city, farms, nearby village, road edge |
| E | starter region, small dungeon, nearby routes |
| D | multiple towns, dangerous roads, ruins, specialist zones |
| C | wider province, faction outposts, major regional crises |
| B | national travel, capitals, border operations |
| A | foreign countries, frontier territories, international expeditions |
| S | forbidden regions, unknown nodes, lost civilizations, world anomalies |

This is not a hard invisible wall. Legal, logistical and social access explain most gating.

---

# 10. Travel should be gameplay

Long travel can involve:

- route choice;
- weather;
- food;
- camp;
- rumors;
- roadside settlements;
- convoy work;
- social conversations;
- discoveries;
- optional detours.

Do not require the player to physically walk empty real-time distances just to prove the world is large.

---

# 11. Revisit value

Old locations should remain useful after Rank S.

Possible reasons:

- festivals;
- relationships;
- housing;
- crafting specialists;
- personal history;
- rare seasonal events;
- teaching beginners;
- new clues revealed by later knowledge;
- economic opportunity;
- hidden layers in familiar places.

A legendary player should still have reasons to return to the starter Guild.
